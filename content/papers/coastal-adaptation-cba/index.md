##### Abstract

In this paper, we develop a methodology to assess the costs and benefits of climate adaptation policies for coastal assets, applied to regions in Santa Cruz and Ventura, California. We classify assets affected by flooding and estimate their economic losses using methods appropriate for each asset type including hedonic pricing, rent-to-revenue imputation and input output analysis. Using flood scenarios from the CoSMoS Adapts model, we account for uncertainty in potential losses and estimate how much people would be willing to pay to reduce these risks. We compare these estimates with the costs of six adaptation strategies: seawalls, beach nourishment, levees, groins, managed retreat and dune restoration. We then calculate cost-benefit ratios using both conventional methods and estimates that account for uncertainty and risk. Our findings show that accounting for uncertainty can change the assessment of which adaptation strategies offer the greatest economic benefits compared to conventional approaches with implications for adaptation finance and policy design.
