##### Abstract

Public agencies and local governments increasingly use benefit-cost analysis (BCA) to decide which climate adaptation projects to fund. Standard BCA models value adaptation almost entirely through avoided direct physical damage evaluated at expected values. This approach risks underestimating benefits along two dimensions: spillovers from direct damages and reduction in uncertainty of the damage distribution.

We develop a general, hazard-agnostic framework that (i) organizes losses into a common ledger of assets, loss channels, and stakeholders, with explicit rules against double counting; (ii) assigns each loss channel a valuation method that can be implemented with public data in any U.S. jurisdiction in order to evaluate spillover effects; (iii) converts hazard-model output into a full annual loss distribution rather than a single expected value; and (iv) values the reduction in risk itself through an option-price measure that reflects both avoided expected losses and a reduction in uncertainty borne by those who cannot diversify the loss. The framework can also accommodate co-benefits and dis-benefits of adaptation, time-varying hazard, and distributional weights. We pilot the framework with physics-based storm-flood scenarios from the CoSMoS-Adapt model in Santa Cruz County, California, evaluating damage-reduction benefits of dune restoration, submerged reefs, and seawalls. In this pilot, our framework increases estimated benefits by a factor of three to five relative to standard BCA models.
