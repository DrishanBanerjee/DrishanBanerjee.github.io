##### Abstract

Does religiosity shape the relationship between financial development and economic growth? Using cross-country panel data, this paper examines whether the growth effects of financialization depend on how religious a society is. We build on previous work that establishes a non-linear relationship between financial development and growth and find that this relationship is present in advanced economies and secular emerging economies: beyond a certain point, financialization is associated with lower growth. This pattern does not hold in more devout emerging economies even at similar levels of financialization. The findings suggest that the relationship between finance and growth depends not only on the extent of financial development but also on the social context.
